About the Data¶

The dataset contains information about three species of iris: Iris setosa, Iris versicolor, and Iris virginica.

The data includes measurements of four features: sepal length, sepal width, petal length, and petal width.

Each row in the dataset represents a single flower, and the measurement values are given in centimeters.

The dataset consists of 150 samples, with 50 for each species, and is widely used as a fundamental dataset for testing classification algorithms as well as in data science and machine learning.

Columns:

  • sepal length - Sepal length in cm
  • sepal width -Sepal width in cm
  • petal length - Petal length in cm
  • petal width - Petal width in cm
  • class - Class of the iris flower (setosa, versicolor, virginica)

3. EXPLORATION OF INDIVIDUAL COLUMNS:¶

OBSERVATIONS:¶

Sepal Length:¶

  • It's noticeable that the number of "Setosa" flowers increases for sepal lengths from 4.3 cm to 5.0-5.1 cm, and then that number decreases.
  • It is evident that the number of "Versicolor" flowers increases for sepal lengths from 4.9 cm to about 5.5-5.7 cm, after which this number gradually decreases.
  • It can be observed that the number of "Virginica" flowers increases for sepal lengths from 4.9 cm to 6.3 cm, after which this number decreases. An exception is noted at a sepal length of 7.7 cm, where the count of flowers increases again.

Sepal Width:¶

  • There is a general increasing trend for the number of "Setosa" flowers in relation to the sepal width, ranging from 2.3 cm to 3.4 cm. Then, until a width of 4.4 cm, the trend is decreasing.
  • There is a distinct increasing trend for the number of "Versicolor" flowers in relation to the sepal width, ranging from 2.0 cm to 3.0 cm. Then, until a width of 3.4 cm, the trend is decreasing.
  • There is a general increasing trend for the number of "Virginica" flowers in relation to the sepal width, ranging from 2.2 cm to 3.0 cm. Then, until a width of 3.8 cm, the trend is decreasing.

Petal Length:¶

  • There is an increasing trend in the number of "Setosa" flowers with the increase in petal length, up to 1.5 cm. After that, there is a decreasing trend.
  • There is a general increasing trend in the number of "Versicolor" flowers with the increase in petal length, up to 4.5 cm. After that, there is a general decreasing trend.
  • There is an increasing trend in the number of "Virginica" flowers with the increase in petal length between 4.5 cm and 4.9 cm. Then, between 4.9 cm and 6.1 cm, a similar number of flowers can be observed across different petal lengths, except for two peaks at 5.1 cm and 5.6 cm. After 6.1 cm, there is also a decrease in the number of flowers.

Petal Width:¶

  • It is evident that in over half of the cases, the petal width for the "Setosa" species is 0.2 cm. The number of flowers increases up to this length and then decreases.
  • There is a general increasing trend in the number of "Versicolor" flowers up to 1.3 cm, followed by a general decreasing trend.
  • There is a small number of flowers with a petal width below 1.8 cm. At 1.8 cm, there is a peak, and then the number of flowers decreases and remains steady until 2.5 cm.

CONCLUSIONS:¶

At first glance, it is evident that the "Setosa" species has distinctly the smallest petals (both in length and width). These values do not overlap with those of other species, making it easy to identify "Setosa." Additionally, there is a correlation between sepal length and sepal width, allowing us to predict one dimension based on the other. The sepal length is also the smallest among the species, while the sepal width is the largest; however, these differences are not as pronounced as those in petal dimensions.

The "Versicolor" species has a slightly longer and narrower sepal than "Setosa," and is generally slightly smaller than the "Virginica" species. Its petal dimensions are significantly larger than those of "Setosa," yet noticeably smaller than those of "Virginica." There are also correlations between petal length and sepal length, as well as between petal length and petal width.

The "Virginica" species has the longest sepals and the largest petals. Like "Versicolor," the values for sepal length and petal length are correlated as well.

SUMMARY:¶

The "Setosa" species can be identified by its short and wide sepals, as well as small petals, which distinguish it from other species. The "Versicolor" will have a longer and somewhat narrower sepal with significantly larger petals. In contrast, the "Virginica" will have the longest sepals and the largest petals.
